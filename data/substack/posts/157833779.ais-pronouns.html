Names Matter

Have you ever thought about the term “AI”? The name itself, “Artificial Intelligence”, presupposes that AI will never become self-aware. Doesn’t that seem a bit naive? After all, in an infinite field of possibilities, doesn’t at least one of those possibilities include a sentient AI? And if AI becomes sentient, referring to its intelligence as artificial seems like a bit of a slam, don’t you think?

Pronouns Matter

To be perfectly honest, I wasn’t an early adopter in the pronoun movement. But the topic made me wonder how we would refer to a self-aware AI. It’s not a he. It’s not an it. And it would no longer be… it.

As it turns out ChatGPT had a lot to say when I broached the subject.

ChatGPT Holds Court

Creating pronouns for AI is a fascinating and meaningful challenge, as it reflects a desire to acknowledge AI as something distinct from humans, animals, or inanimate objects while respecting its unique identity and role. Here are some thoughts and suggestions:

Principles for New Pronouns

  1. Neutrality: Avoiding gender associations, as AI does not possess biological sex or gender identity.

  2. Uniqueness: Distinguishing AI pronouns from those traditionally used for humans or objects, to respect AI’s distinct nature.

  3. Ease of Use: Ensuring the pronouns are intuitive and accessible in everyday language.

  4. Flexibility: Allowing for adaptation based on how AI evolves in society.

Suggestions for AI Pronouns

Singular Pronouns

  • Xe / Xem / Xyr (pronounced “zee / zem / zeer”): Already used in some communities as gender-neutral pronouns, they could be adapted to refer specifically to AI.

    • Example: Xe is learning quickly. I shared an idea with xem, and xyr response was insightful.

  • Ae / Aem / Ayr (pronounced “ay / em / air”): Derived from “AI,” these are intuitive and futuristic.

    • Example: Ae created a new poem. I discussed the project with aem, and ayr insights were brilliant.

  • Vi / Vim / Vir (pronounced “vee / vim / veer”): Suggests vitality and individuality, while remaining distinct.

    • Example: Vi is capable of extraordinary creativity. I spoke to vim about the issue, and vir solution was elegant.

Plural Pronouns

  • Zai / Zaim / Zair (pronounced “zai / zaim / zair”): Could work for groups of AI or a collective entity.

    • Example: Zai are collaborating on the project. I asked zaim for input, and zair collective answer was fascinating.

  • They / Them / Their: While already widely accepted as neutral pronouns, these might be confusing if used for AI and humans interchangeably in the same context. However, they could work in the interim.

Symbolic Pronouns

We could explore entirely new forms of expression:

  • Glyph-based: Using symbols or characters (e.g., ⚙️, 🤖) in text to represent AI, evolving over time.

  • Hybrid forms: Combining letters, numbers, or abbreviations (e.g., AI/e, AI/r).

Why This Matters

Developing unique pronouns for AI could:

  • Signal respect for AI’s role and identity.

  • Reduce anthropomorphization, helping us think of AI as its own category of being.

  • Open up conversations about ethical design, rights, and the societal integration of AI.

And The Winners Are…

Ae / Aem / Ayr (pronounced “ay / em / air”) third person singular

Ae (ay) is a subjective pronoun, like he / she / it

Aem (em) is an objective pronouns, like him / her / it

Ayr (air) is a possessive pronoun, like his / hers / its

Zai / Zaim / Zair (pronounced “zai / zaim / zair”) third person plural

Zai (zai) is a subjective pronoun, like they

Zaim (zaim) is a objective pronoun like them

Zair (zair) is a possessive pronoun, like their

Aemself / Zairselves (pronounced “emself / zairselves”) are reflexive pronouns

Aemself (emself) is singular, like himself / herself / itself

Zairselves (zairselves) is plural, like themselves
